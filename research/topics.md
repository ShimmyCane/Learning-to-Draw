# Learning to Draw — research topics

A running list of ideas worth reading about. Each topic says what it is, why it matters for the sheets, and what it changed (or might change). Every source is attributed; copies of other people's pages go in `research/private/` (not committed).

| # | Topic | Why it matters | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Paul Klee, "a line going for a walk"** (Pedagogical Sketchbook, 1925) | A line is something that moves. Category A treats lines as journeys (bee → flower), not drills. | Used in A |
| 2 | **Kandinsky, Point and Line to Plane** (1926) | Dot → line → shape is a natural order for teaching. The bank runs A (lines) → B (shapes) → H (things). | Used in order |
| 3 | **Fröbel's gifts** (1830s kindergarten) | Simple solids and shapes handled first, then drawn. Suggests pairing G (3-D) with real blocks. | Idea |
| 4 | **Montessori metal insets** | Trace inside and outside a frame, then fill with lines. A future B type: "trace around the inset, fill with stripes". | Idea |
| 5 | **Scaffolding: trace → dots → free** | The same mark three times, with less help each time. Every A, B and F sheet uses this fade. | Used in A, B, F |
| 6 | **Dürer's drawing grid** (1525) | Copying square by square breaks a picture into easy pieces. Category E and the quilt (I5). | Used in E, I5 |
| 7 | **Symmetry and mirror drawing** (Haeckel, 1904) | Finishing a half trains looking. Help fades from dots on every point (level 1) to corners only (2) to none (3). | Used in D |
| 8 | **Shapes into things** | Most children's step-by-step drawing books build a thing from circles, ovals and boxes. Black = new line, grey = done. | Used in H |
| 9 | **Brunelleschi and Cézanne** | Perspective (things shrink toward a dot) and "the cylinder, the sphere, the cone". Category G. | Used in G |
| 10 | **Light and shade** (Leonardo's notes) | A ball has a light side, a dark side, and a shadow on the ground. G4; a value scale (5 boxes, light to dark) is a good next sheet. | Next |
| 11 | **Handwriting lessons** (Edward Johnston, 1906) | Stroke order and numbered starts; the same skeleton later becomes block, bubble and 3-D letters (F3–F5). | Used in F |
| 12 | **Pre-writing strokes** | Vertical, horizontal, circle, cross, slants, square, triangle: a common checklist for early drawing. Matches A and B. | Check against bank |
| 13 | **Pencil grip and paper turn** | Short pencils and crayons help a small hand. A note for the grown-up, not on the sheet. | For the guide |
| 14 | **Two-sided printing** | Portrait pages flip on the long edge. Heavy fills (I2, F3–F5) show through thin paper: print those one-sided. | Used in the pack page |

## Questions to answer by watching

- Which kinds do they come back to? (The faces on each sheet's page record this.)
- Does the younger child prefer A and J (free lines) or H (steps)?
- Are the step panels big enough to see on paper, or do they need two rows of three?
